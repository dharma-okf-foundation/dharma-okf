# Primary Source References

The 12 primary sources cited across this bundle's concepts, each a first-class `type: Reference` document so citations resolve inside the bundle (PROFILE.md §3.2).

* [Devī Sūkta (Ṛg Veda 10.125) — देवीसूक्त](devi-sukta.md) - The Vedic anchor of Śākta theology: the Ṛg Vedic hymn in which Vāk (the goddess Word, identified by the tradition with Devī) declares herself the power moving through all gods, the bender of Rudra's bow, and the one who pervades heaven and earth.
* [Devī Upaniṣad — देवी उपनिषद्](devi-upanishad.md) - A principal Śākta Upaniṣad in which the gods ask the Great Goddess who she is and she answers with the Upaniṣadic ultimacy formula: 'I am essentially Brahman' (ahaṃ brahmasvarūpiṇī); the clearest short text uniting Vedāntic vocabulary with Devī theology.
* [Devī Māhātmya — देवीमाहात्म्य (scoped re-reference)](devi-mahatmya.md) - SCOPED RE-REFERENCE: the foundational Śākta scripture (Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa 81-93) already carried as a reference in cosmology-creation v0.7 for its cosmological content; re-referenced here for its darśana content: Mahādevī theology, the ekaivāhaṃ declaration, Kālī's emergence, and the theology of the Goddess's supremacy.
* [Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa — देवीभागवतपुराण](devi-bhagavata-purana.md) - The great Śākta Purāṇa: 12 books systematizing Devī theology, including the Devī Gītā (book 7), the Mahāvidyā origin narrative, and the Purāṇic elaboration of Devī as ultimate reality; the tradition's own claim to Mahāpurāṇa status for a Goddess-centered scripture.
* [Lalitā Sahasranāma — ललितासहस्रनाम](lalita-sahasranama.md) - The thousand names of Lalitā Tripurasundarī (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa): the Śrī-Vidyā tradition's public liturgical core, functioning as systematic theology in name-form: cosmology, the Śrī Cakra, the tattvas, and the goddess's supremacy encoded as a recitable garland of names.
* [Saundarya Laharī — सौन्दर्यलहरी](saundarya-lahari.md) - The Wave of Beauty': 100 verses of Śākta devotional-metaphysical poetry, traditionally attributed to Śaṅkara (attribution contested in scholarship: both positions documented); opens with the bundle's most-cited theological verse (Śiva can act only united with Śakti) and carries Śrī Cakra and mantra-śāstra doctrine in poetic form.
* [Tripurā Rahasya — त्रिपुरारहस्य](tripura-rahasya.md) - The Mystery of Tripurā': the Śākta tradition's great jñāna text, framed as Dattātreya teaching Paraśurāma: consciousness (as the Goddess Tripurā) as sole reality, taught through philosophical dialogue and teaching stories; the standing proof that Śākta tradition has a fully developed knowledge-path register.
* [Spanda Kārikās — स्पन्दकारिका](spanda-karikas.md) - The 53 verses founding the Spanda school of Kashmir Shaivism: the doctrine of absolute consciousness as creative pulsation (spanda), attributed to Vasugupta or his disciple Kallaṭa, with Kṣemarāja's Spanda-nirṇaya as the major commentary.
* [Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam — प्रत्यभिज्ञाहृदयम्](pratyabhijnahridayam.md) - The Heart of Recognition': Kṣemarāja's 20 sūtras with auto-commentary, the classic compact statement of the Pratyabhijñā doctrine: citi (consciousness-power) as the free cause of the universe, its self-contraction as each knower, and liberation as recognition; written explicitly as the accessible digest of Utpaladeva's system.
* [Tantrāloka — तन्त्रालोक (structural citation)](tantraloka.md) - STRUCTURAL CITATION ONLY: Abhinavagupta's 'Light on the Tantras', the encyclopedic synthesis of Trika Śaivism (37 āhnikas covering doctrine, the 36 tattvas, śaktipāta, dīkṣā, ritual, and aesthetics), cited in this bundle at the level of its architecture and doctrinal positions; its ritual and upāsana content is deliberately not documented.
* [Kulārṇava Tantra — कुलार्णवतन्त्र (descriptive citation)](kularnava-tantra.md) - DESCRIPTIVE CITATION ONLY: a principal Kaula tantra, the locus classicus for guru-dīkṣā doctrine, the ācāra classifications, and the regulated left-current framework INCLUDING its own internal warnings; cited in this bundle as the adhikāra-discipline exemplar; its ritual content is not documented.
* [Ṣaṭcakranirūpaṇa — षट्चक्रनिरूपण](shatchakranirupana.md) - The Examination of the Six Cakras' (Pūrṇānanda, 16th c.): the text that became the world's chakra doctrine: the six-plus-crown scheme with petals, phonemes, bījas, tattvas, and deities; included in this bundle BOTH as the primary Ṣaṭcakra source AND as the error-genealogy exhibit: Arthur Avalon's 1919 'The Serpent Power' translated this text, and its flawed rendering seeded the Western kundalini-chakra construct.
