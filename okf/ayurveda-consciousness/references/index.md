# Primary Source References

The 8 primary sources cited across this bundle's concepts, each a first-class `type: Reference` document so citations resolve inside the bundle (PROFILE.md §3.2).

* [Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya (Vāgbhaṭa)](ashtanga-hridaya.md) - Vāgbhaṭa synthesis of the Charaka and Suśruta traditions in polished verse, the standard teaching text, source of the tridoṣa summary, the daily and seasonal regimens, and the formation of āma.
* [Aṣṭāṅga Saṃgraha (Vāgbhaṭa)](ashtanga-samgraha.md) - The longer prose-and-verse companion of the Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, expanding the same synthesis of the Charaka and Suśruta traditions across the eight limbs of medicine.
* [Bhāvaprakāśa](bhavaprakasha.md) - The 16th-century compendium of Bhāvamiśra, bridge of the classical system toward early modern practice, with its celebrated glossary (nighaṇṭu) and restatements of constitution and regimen.
* [Charaka Saṃhitā](charaka-samhita.md) - The foundational compendium of internal medicine (kāyacikitsā), redacted by Charaka from the Agniveśa Tantra, source of the definitions of āyus, svastha conduct, prajñāpāradha, sattvāvajaya, and the therapeutic architecture this bundle cites most.
* [Kāśyapa Saṃhitā](kashyapa-samhita.md) - The classical compendium of pediatrics and obstetrics (kaumārabhṛtya), witness to the breadth of the eight-limbed medicine and to constitution and nourishment doctrine applied from conception onward.
* [Mādhava Nidāna](madhava-nidana.md) - The classical manual of diagnosis and pathology (nidāna), standard reference for disease definition and the staging of pathogenesis the doṣa-āma-srotas vocabulary of this bundle feeds into.
* [Śārṅgadhara Saṃhitā](sarngadhara-samhita.md) - The concise later compendium prized for pharmacy and physiology summaries, including the popular exposition of the doṣa clock of day, night, and life stages.
* [Suśruta Saṃhitā](sushruta-samhita.md) - The foundational compendium of the surgical school, source of the canonical positive definition of health (svastha, Sūtrasthāna 15.41), the doctrine of para and apara ojas, and the five-fold enumeration of purification that includes bloodletting.
