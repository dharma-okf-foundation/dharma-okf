# Concepts

The 26 concept documents in this directory, in the order and groupings of the bundle index. Each is a `type: Concept` document carrying `not:` and `darshana:` (PROFILE.md §2.1, §2.2).

## Tier 1 -- CRITICAL: Kāla (Time System)

* [Kāla — काल](kala.md) - Cosmic time as a primordial principle and deity; not linear clock-time but the cyclic, all-consuming force that governs manifestation, measures Yugas and Kalpas, and is identical with Mahākāla (Śiva as the great destroyer); time that devours all things while being itself uncaused.
* [Yuga — युग](yuga.md) - One of four cosmic ages (Satya/Kṛta, Tretā, Dvāpara, Kali) constituting the Caturyuga cycle; each Yuga has a defined duration, a characteristic quality of dharma (measured in pādas/legs), and a prescribed spiritual practice; the present Kali Yuga began at Kṛṣṇa's departure (~3102 BCE per traditional reckoning).
* [Mahāyuga / Caturyuga — महायुग / चतुर्युग](mahayuga.md) - The complete four-Yuga cycle (Satya + Tretā + Dvāpara + Kali = 4,320,000 human years) constituting the fundamental unit of cosmic time from which all larger Purāṇic time-scales are built; also called Caturyuga ('four-Yuga cycle') or Divya-Yuga; 1000 Mahāyugas = one Kalpa (day of Brahmā).
* [Kalpa — कल्प](kalpa.md) - The cosmological day of Brahmā, equal to 1000 Mahāyugas (4,320,000,000 human years); the largest regularly recurring unit of cosmic time, at the end of which a Naimittika Pralaya dissolves the three lower worlds; distinguished from Kalpa as Vedāṅga (ritual procedure texts).
* [Manvantara — मन्वन्तर](manvantara.md) - The cosmic epoch of a Manu (progenitor-archetype), comprising 71 Mahāyugas (~306.7 million years); 14 Manvantaras constitute one Kalpa; each has its own Manu, Indra, saptarṣis, and Viṣṇu-Avatāra; the current epoch is the 7th Manvantara (Vaivasvata), whose Manu is the source of the present human lineage.

## Tier 2 -- HIGH: The Four Cosmic Functions

* [Sṛṣṭi — सृष्टि](srishti.md) - Cosmic emanation: the projection of the manifest cosmos from Brahman/Avyakta; NOT creation ex nihilo (from nothing) but the drawing-out (sṛj = to emit, project, release) of what was already present in unmanifest form; one of the three eternal cosmic functions alongside Sthiti (maintenance) and Saṃhāra (dissolution).
* [Sthiti — स्थिति](sthiti.md) - Cosmic maintenance: the active sustaining of the manifest cosmos during a Kalpa; Viṣṇu's primary function in the Trimūrti schema; not passive conservation but the continuous upholding of the conditions for dharma and liberation to operate; the middle term of the Sṛṣṭi-Sthiti-Saṃhāra triad.
* [Saṃhāra / Laya — संहार / लय](samhara.md) - Cosmic withdrawal or reabsorption: the drawing-back of the manifest cosmos into its unmanifest source at the end of a cosmic cycle; Śiva's function in the Trimūrti schema; NOT destruction or annihilation — nothing is lost, everything is reabsorbed; distinguished from Pralaya, which is the resulting state after Saṃhāra.
* [Pralaya — प्रलय](pralaya.md) - Cosmic dissolution: the state of non-manifestation that follows Saṃhāra; four types at different scales (Nitya, Naimittika, Mahā, Ātyantika); NOT universal annihilation -- the causal seeds of beings persist through all but the final type; the most critically misrepresented concept in Purāṇic cosmology because all four types are routinely collapsed into one.

## Tier 3 -- HIGH: Cosmological Agents

* [Trimūrti — त्रिमूर्ति](trimurti.md) - The triple-form of the supreme cosmic principle manifesting as three functional agents: Brahmā (Sṛṣṭi / creation), Viṣṇu (Sthiti / maintenance), Śiva (Saṃhāra / withdrawal); a functional schema for the cosmic cycle, not a pantheon of three rival gods; often misread as equivalent to the Christian Trinity or as three separate 'gods.
* [Brahmā (The Creator) — ब्रह्मा](brahma-the-creator.md) - The four-faced cosmic agent of Sṛṣṭi (emanation); the secondary creator who differentiates and populates the cosmos within each Kalpa; himself born at the beginning of each Kalpa from Hiraṇyagarbha / Viṣṇu's navel; he will dissolve at Mahā Pralaya; CRITICALLY NOT Brahman (the unconditioned absolute) -- the most dangerous transliteration conflation in all of Dharmic AI output.
* [Avatāra — अवतार](avatara.md) - The intentional descent of the divine into the manifest world for a specific cosmic purpose; primarily Viṣṇu's self-projection into conditioned existence to restore dharma within the Sthiti (maintenance) function; NOT reincarnation; NOT the same as a jīva taking rebirth; the descent is of the infinite into the finite, not the finite migrating between finite forms.
* [Īśvara (Cosmological) — ईश्वर](ishvara-cosmological.md) - The lord or ruler: Brahman as the intelligent cosmic controller operating through Māyā; the personal aspect of the absolute that generates, maintains, and dissolves the cosmos; the bridge between the impersonal Brahman (Nirguṇa) and the personal cosmic ruler (Saguṇa); NOT the same as the Abrahamic God; treated here in its cosmological dimension (as cosmic controller of Sṛṣṭi-Sthiti-Saṃhāra) rather than in its soteriological dimension (as the object of bhakti).

## Tier 4 -- MEDIUM-HIGH: Cosmic Structures

* [Hiraṇyagarbha — हिरण्यगर्भ](hiranyagarbha.md) - The 'golden womb' or 'golden embryo': the first manifestation from the unmanifest Avyakta; the subtle-cosmic body of the universe; the totality of all subtle existences before gross differentiation; the Ṛg Vedic cosmic principle named in RV 10.121; equated with Brahmā-as-cosmic-egg in Purāṇic accounts and with Sūtrātman (the thread-ātman) in Vedāntic analysis.
* [Virāṭ — विराट्](virat.md) - The 'vast' or 'expanded' cosmic form: the gross-cosmic totality that emerges from Hiraṇyagarbha; the universe considered as one differentiated, embodied whole; the cosmic-waking-state consciousness (Vaiśvānara at the macro level); the stage of Sṛṣṭi at which the universe becomes the grossly differentiated field within which Brahmā operates; described in its most famous form in the Puruṣa Sūkta and the Viśvarūpa of BG 11.
* [Brahmāṇḍa — ब्रह्माण्ड](brahmanda.md) - The 'egg of Brahmā': the cosmic shell or container of the manifest universe, comprising all 14 Lokas arranged vertically within a single cosmic unit; the structured physical-cosmological space within which a Kalpa's life unfolds; multiple Brahmāṇḍas exist simultaneously (Purāṇic accounts vary on this); NOT the same as the universe in the modern astrophysical sense.
* [Sapta-Loka / Chaturdaśa-Loka — सप्त-लोक / चतुर्दश-लोक](sapta-loka.md) - The seven upper realms (or the full fourteen-realm system of Chaturdaśa-Loka) constituting the Purāṇic cosmological map of the Brahmāṇḍa; NOT a literal physical geography but a map of states of existence organized by consciousness-quality, dharmic state, and karmic position; Bhūr (earth) is the third realm, not the lowest -- the seven lower realms (Pātāla) exist below it.

## Tier 5 -- MEDIUM-HIGH: Foundational Texts and Principles

* [Avyakta — अव्यक्त](avyakta.md) - The Unmanifest: the undifferentiated total potential of the manifest cosmos, prior to the emergence of Hiraṇyagarbha; the state of the cosmos during Pralaya when all manifest content has been reabsorbed; in Sāṃkhya, Avyakta = Prakṛti in its primordial undifferentiated state (also called Pradhāna / Mūlaprakṛti); in Vedānta, Avyakta is Brahman's creative power (Māyā-Śakti) in its dormant phase -- NOT the same as Brahman itself.
* [Puruṣa Sūkta — पुरुष सूक्त](purusha-sukta.md) - The Hymn of the Cosmic Person (Ṛg Veda 10.90): the foundational Vedic cosmogonic text describing the primordial Puruṣa (Cosmic Person) whose sacrificial dismemberment generates the cosmos; establishes the macrocosm-microcosm identity; presents the varṇa schema as cosmic in origin; one of the most widely cited and most frequently misread texts in Vedic literature.
* [Nāsadīya Sūkta — नासदीय सूक्त](nasadiya-sukta.md) - The Hymn of Non-Existence / Hymn of Creation (Ṛg Veda 10.129): the most philosophically radical of the Vedic cosmogonic hymns; it suspends all categorical answers about the origin of the cosmos and questions whether even the gods know how it arose; its final verse poses the possibility that even the one who oversees the cosmos from above 'may not know' -- making it a canonical ancient example of intellectual humility about first causes.
* [Triguṇas (Cosmological) — त्रिगुण](trigunas-cosmological.md) - The three fundamental strands (guṇas: Sattva / Rajas / Tamas) of Prakṛti treated in their cosmological dimension: as the constitutive qualities of manifest existence from Avyakta through all 23 tattvas; as the principle by which the Trimūrti's three cosmic functions are distributed; as the organizing force of the Yuga cycle (Satya-Yuga being Sattva-dominant, Kali-Yuga being Tamas-increasing); v0.7 treats the cosmological dimension; the yoga-darshana bundle (v0.4) treats the epistemological/psychological dimension.

## Tier 6 -- MEDIUM: Śākta-Tāntric Cosmological Principles

* [Pañcamahābhūta (Cosmological) — पञ्चमहाभूत](panchamahabhuta-cosmological.md) - The five great elements (Ākāśa/space, Vāyu/air, Tejas/fire, Ap/water, Pṛthivī/earth) in their cosmological dimension: as the terminal products of the Sāṃkhya tattva sequence, arising from the five subtle elements (tanmātras) through the process of Pañcīkaraṇa (fivefold combination); they constitute all gross physical matter and generate the five sense capacities; v0.7 treats the full derivation sequence; dharma-foundation bundle gives a brief introduction only.
* [Māyā (Cosmological) — माया](maya-cosmological.md) - The cosmic creative power of Brahman by which the unmanifest absolute appears as the differentiated manifest cosmos; Mahāmāyā operating at the level of Brahman's own nature; distinct from Māyā as individual veil (avidyā), which operates at the level of the individual jīva's ignorance; v0.7 treats the cosmological Māyā (Mahāmāyā); upanishadic-core v0.5 treats the individual Māyā/avidyā dimension.
* [Śakti (Cosmological) — शक्ति](shakti-cosmological.md) - The cosmic power or creative energy of the divine: at the cosmological level, Śakti is the dynamic principle by which the unchanging absolute (Brahman / Śiva) manifests as the dynamic cosmos; in Śākta tradition, Śakti IS the supreme reality; in Vaiṣṇava tradition, Śakti = Lakṣmī or Yogamāyā as Viṣṇu's power; in Kashmir Shaivism, Śiva-Śakti as consciousness-power are inseparable; treated here in its cosmological dimension (as the power of manifestation); the triad of Śakti's cosmological modes -- Icchā, Jñāna, Kriyā -- is treated separately in iccha-jnana-kriya-shakti.md.
* [Līlā (Cosmological) — लीला](lila-cosmological.md) - The cosmic play or sport: at the cosmological level, Līlā is the answer to the question 'why does Brahman create at all?' -- the cosmos arises not from necessity, compulsion, deficiency, or purpose external to Brahman, but as the free, spontaneous, joyful self-expression of infinite consciousness; at this level Līlā grounds all of existence as intrinsically purposeless yet intrinsically meaningful; the relational and devotional dimension of Līlā (Kṛṣṇa's Vṛndāvana play) is treated in bhakti-marga v0.5.
* [Icchā-Jñāna-Kriyā Śakti — इच्छा-ज्ञान-क्रिया शक्ति](iccha-jnana-kriya-shakti.md) - The triad of Śiva's (or Brahman's) three primary Śaktis: Icchā-Śakti (will-power / the impulse to create), Jñāna-Śakti (knowledge-power / the omniscient awareness that knows what to create and how), and Kriyā-Śakti (action-power / the executive force that actualizes creation); together they constitute the complete internal cosmological mechanism by which the absolute moves from unmanifest potential to manifest cosmos; the triad is treated in full in Kashmir Shaivism and Śaiva Siddhānta but is recognizable across Vaiṣṇava and Vedāntic frameworks as well.
