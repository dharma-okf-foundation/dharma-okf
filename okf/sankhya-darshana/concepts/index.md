# Concepts

The 20 concept documents in this directory, in the order and groupings of the bundle index. Each is a `type: Concept` document carrying `not:` and `darshana:` (PROFILE.md §2.1, §2.2).

## Tier 1 -- Core Dualism & Causation (CRITICAL)

* [Puruṣa](purusha.md) - Pure, inactive consciousness -- the passive witness-principle of Sāṃkhya, explicitly PLURAL (countless individual puruṣas, no hierarchy among them) and NOT the same as Vedāntic ātman/brahman, NOT a soul that acts, NOT Vaiśeṣika ātman (which is a substance bearing qualities).
* [Prakṛti](prakriti.md) - Primordial, unconscious, ever-active matter/nature -- the sole material cause of the entire manifest world, NOT 'Nature' in a modern ecological sense, and NOT the same prakriti already gestured at in dharma-foundation (v0.1) as a loose synonym for 'nature'; here it is a precise metaphysical category with two named phases, avyakta (unmanifest) and vyakta (manifest).
* [Satkāryavāda](satkaryavada.md) - The doctrine that the effect pre-exists, latently, in its material cause -- the signature Samkhya causal theory (shared in modified form by Yoga and Vedanta), directly OPPOSED to Nyaya-Vaisheshika's asatkaryavada/arambhavada (v0.9), which holds the effect is a wholly new entity.
* [Pariṇāmavāda](parinamavada.md) - The specific mechanism by which satkaryavada operates: real transformation of the cause into the effect (not mere appearance) -- distinguished from Advaita Vedanta''s vivartavada (v0.3), where the effect is an apparent, not a real, transformation of the cause.
* [Vyakta-Avyakta](vyakta-avyakta.md) - The two phases of prakriti -- avyakta, the unmanifest root-state of guna-equilibrium, and vyakta, the manifest, differentiated world -- named here as the minimal structural concept needed to state prakriti's causal role, NOT a restatement of the full 24-tattva evolution sequence (already published across yoga-darshana v0.2, cosmology-creation v0.7, and ayurveda-consciousness v0.11).

## Tier 2 -- Guṇa Apparatus, Source Framing (HIGH)

* [Guṇa (Sāṃkhya)](guna-samkhya.md) - The three constituent strands of prakriti -- sattva, rajas, tamas -- whose dynamic equilibrium and disturbance constitute all of manifest reality. This is guna''s DOCTRINAL SOURCE: the term appears downstream, as an applied or cross-referenced concept, in bhakti-marga (v0.4), cosmology-creation (v0.7), nyaya-vaisheshika (v0.9, an unrelated ontological sense), and ayurveda-consciousness (v0.11). This file is the origin point, not another peer usage.
* [Sattva](sattva.md) - The guna of lucidity, clarity, and manifestation -- one of the three constituent strands of prakriti, NOT a synonym for 'goodness' in a simple moral sense, and NOT the same word as sattva/sat meaning 'existence' or 'being' used elsewhere in the corpus.
* [Rajas](rajas.md) - The guna of activity, motion, and restlessness -- the constituent responsible for change and effort in prakriti, paired here with sattva; tamas is treated within guna-samkhya's body rather than as a fourth standalone file, keeping this tier tight per the concept-lock's distinctiveness discipline.

## Tier 3 -- Soteriology (HIGH)

* [Viveka-Khyāti](viveka-khyati.md) - Discriminative knowledge -- the specifically Samkhya METHOD of liberation: intellectual discernment of purusha''s absolute distinctness from prakriti (including buddhi, ahamkara, manas). NOT the same method as Yoga''s path via nirodha (cessation of mental modifications through the eight limbs) -- same end-state word (kaivalya), different method.
* [Kaivalya (Sāṃkhya)](kaivalya-samkhya.md) - Absolute isolation of purusha from prakriti -- Samkhya''s term for final liberation, SCHOOL-SCOPED against yoga-darshana''s already-published kaivalya (v0.2): same word, same general sense (isolation/aloneness), but reached by a different method (viveka-khyati here vs. nirodha there) and without any Ishvara-inclusive framing.
* [Avidyā (Sāṃkhya)](avidya-samkhya.md) - Non-discrimination -- the specifically Samkhya sense of ignorance: the failure to distinguish purusha from prakriti (especially from buddhi), the root cause of bondage. SCHOOL-SCOPED against upanishadic-core''s avidya (v0.6), which operates within a non-dual, single-Self framework rather than Samkhya''s dualist one.
* [Bandha (Sāṃkhya)](bandha-samkhya.md) - Bondage -- the apparent (not literal) condition of purusha appearing entangled in prakriti's activity due to non-discrimination, NOT a real change undergone by purusha itself, and NOT a punitive or moral state.

## Tier 4 -- Epistemology & Method (MEDIUM-HIGH)

* [Pramāṇa (Sāṃkhya)](pramana-samkhya.md) - Samkhya's three-pramana epistemology -- pratyaksha (perception), anumana (inference), and shabda/aptavachana (verbal testimony) -- SCHOOL-SCOPED as the third referent after vedanta-epistemology's Advaita pramanas (v0.3) and nyaya-vaisheshika's Nyaya pramanas (v0.9). Same functional category, different school-specific enumeration and epistemic weighting.
* [Anumāna (Sāṃkhya)](anumana-samkhya.md) - Inference as Samkhya treats it -- brief, school-scoped pointer noting Samkhya's specific classification of inference types, the FOURTH cross-bundle referent for this term after vedanta-epistemology (v0.3, Advaita), nyaya-vaisheshika (v0.9, the lead technical treatment), and Samkhya's own use of inference to argue for the unmanifest (avyakta) and purusha's existence.
* [Kapila](kapila.md) - The traditional founder-sage of the Samkhya school -- a semi-legendary figure credited with the earliest systematization of Samkhya doctrine, distinct from any deity or avatara named Kapila in Puranic literature.
* [Sāṃkhya (as Enumeration)](samkhya-as-enumeration.md) - The school's own self-naming logic -- 'samkhya' means enumeration/counting, referring to the school's method of exhaustively enumerating the tattvas (categories of reality) as the path to discriminative knowledge. This concept treats the METHOD of enumeration, not the tattva ladder's content itself (already published elsewhere).

## Tier 5 -- Historical Strata & School Character (MEDIUM)

* [Nirīśvara](nirishvara.md) - Without-Ishvara -- classical Samkhya''s atheistic stance: no creator deity is philosophically required or admitted, argued textually at Samkhya-Pravacana-Sutra 1.92 (''Ishvara is unproved''). This is the PRIMARY framing for this bundle; Sesvara (theistic) Samkhya is a distinct, later-associated strand, named-and-deferred (see sesvara-samkhya.md).
* [Seśvara Sāṃkhya](sesvara-samkhya.md) - Theistic Samkhya -- a distinct, less prominent historical strand that incorporates Ishvara as an ideal Purusha to emulate (not a creator), named-and-deferred here as a future-bundle pointer (following the v0.9 Navya-Nyaya precedent) rather than given full treatment, to keep this bundle's primary nirishvara framing clean.
* [Sāṃkhya-Kārikā (as concept)](samkhya-karika.md) - The primary text of classical Samkhya -- 72 teaching stanzas by Isvarakrishna (c. 4th-5th century CE), treated here as an index-anchor concept naming the text's role and structure, complementary to its full Reference entry.
* [Sāṃkhya-Pravacana-Sūtra (as concept)](samkhya-pravacana-sutra.md) - The secondary sutra-collection text grounding Samkhya's nirishvara doctrine explicitly (1.92, 'Ishvara is unproved') -- a later compilation attributed to the Kapila tradition, treated here as an index-anchor concept, complementary to its full Reference entry.
