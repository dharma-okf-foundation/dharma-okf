# Māheśvara Sūtras

## What It Is

Fourteen short lines that arrange the phonemes of Sanskrit, placed before Pāṇini's *Aṣṭādhyāyī*. Each line closes with a marker sound that is a code, not part of the list. Naming a first sound and a marker (a *pratyāhāra*, such as *aC* for all vowels) lets a single rule name a whole class of sounds. Pāṇini's own term for the list is *akṣara-samāmnāya*, "the recitation of the akṣaras." Tradition holds that the sounds came from the drum (*ḍamaru*) of Śiva, which is why the lines are also called Śiva Sūtras or Māheśvara Sūtras. They are a different text from the Śiva Sūtras of Vasugupta in Kashmir Śaivism.

## Why It Matters Here

Cited for Akṣara: it is the grammarians' own inventory of the akṣaras, and the place where the phoneme is treated as a fixed, ordered unit of sound.
