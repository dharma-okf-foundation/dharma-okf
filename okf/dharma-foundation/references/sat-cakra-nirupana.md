# Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa

## What It Is

"The description of the six cakras": the sixth chapter of the *Śrītattvacintāmaṇi* of Pūrṇānanda, a tantric author of the sixteenth century. It describes the cakras from Mūlādhāra to Ājñā along the *suṣumnā nāḍī*, then the thousand-petalled lotus above them. For each cakra it gives the number of petals and the Sanskrit letters on them, the presiding deity and the element. Manuscripts also give it other names, such as *Ṣaṭcakrabheda*. Sir John Woodroffe (Arthur Avalon) translated it in *The Serpent Power* (1919).

## Why It Matters Here

Cited for Chakra: it is the Sanskrit source for petals carrying akṣaras, and the source that the 1919 English reception reworked (see the Error Genealogy in the Chakra concept).
