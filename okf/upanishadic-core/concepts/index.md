# Concepts

The 26 concept documents in this directory, in the order and groupings of the bundle index. Each is a `type: Concept` document carrying `not:` and `darshana:` (PROFILE.md §2.1, §2.2).

## Tier 1 — Mahāvākyas (Great Sayings)

* [Mahāvākya](mahavakya.md) - The four 'great sayings' of the Upaniṣads — direct realization-statements, each drawn from one of the four Vedas, that the prepared student recognizes as a disclosure of their own non-dual nature; not theological axioms, sacred formulas, or propositions to be believed.
* [Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi](aham-brahmasmi.md) - The Yajurvedic Mahāvākya from Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.10 — 'I am Brahman' — a direct realization-statement in which the 'I' is witness awareness, not the ego; not a claim that a person is God, not pantheism, not an affirmation.
* [Tat Tvam Asi](tat-tvam-asi.md) - The Sāmavedic Mahāvākya from Chāndogya 6.8.7 — 'That thou art' — a second-person realization-statement in which Brahman (Tat) and the student's innermost self (Tvam) are declared identical; not 'you are the universe,' not panentheism, not a divine spark doctrine.
* [Prajñānam Brahma](prajnanam-brahma.md) - The Ṛgvedic Mahāvākya from Aitareya Upaniṣad 3.3 — 'Consciousness is Brahman' — a definition-statement that identifies Brahman through its intrinsic nature (Svarūpa Lakṣaṇa) as consciousness; not 'intelligence is omniscient,' not idealism, not a description of Brahman as an object.
* [Ayam Ātmā Brahma](ayam-atma-brahma.md) - The Atharvanic Mahāvākya from Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 1.2 — 'This self is Brahman' — the nearest-demonstrative declaration linking Ātman to Brahman through the Avasthātraya (three-states) inquiry; not 'the self is God,' not a claim about the ego, not 'all selves are identical.

## Tier 2 — Triple Method (Śravaṇa-Manana-Nididhyāsana)

* [Śravaṇa](shravana.md) - The first stage of Brahma-jijñāsā — formal, receptive hearing of the Upaniṣadic teaching from a qualified Guru — the pramāṇa-event in which Śabda (verbal testimony) delivers direct Brahma-jñāna; not listening, not studying, not reading, not exposure to content.
* [Manana](manana.md) - The second stage of Brahma-jijñāsā — sustained reflective inquiry that systematically removes doubts (saṃśaya) arising after Śravaṇa — the dialectical clearing of intellectual obstacles to the Upaniṣadic teaching; not meditation, not memorization, not contemplation in the popular sense.
* [Nididhyāsana](nididhyasana.md) - The third stage of Brahma-jijñāsā — deep, sustained internalization of the Mahāvākya's recognition until contrary-to-knowledge saṃskāras are dissolved and the knowledge becomes unobstructed; not concentration, not visualization, not dhyāna in the Yoga-darśana sense.
* [Neti Neti](neti-neti.md) - The apophatic inquiry method of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (2.3.6; 3.9.26; 4.2.4) — 'not this, not this' — a systematic via negativa that removes all conditional predicates from Brahman until what cannot be negated remains; not nihilism, not skepticism, not Western theological apophasis.

## Tier 3 — Four States and Sākṣin

* [Avasthātraya](avastha-traya.md) - The three experiential states — Jāgrat (waking), Svapna (dream), Suṣupti (deep sleep) — as the complete field of possible experience used in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad's inquiry into the witness; not a hierarchy of consciousness, not levels of reality, not sequential stages.
* [Jāgrat](jagrat.md) - The waking state — one of the three states (Avasthātraya) in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad's inquiry — in which the experiencer (Viśva) operates through the gross body and external senses; not privileged as 'objective reality,' not more real than Svapna from the standpoint of the inquiry.
* [Svapna](svapna.md) - The dream state — the second of the three states (Avasthātraya) — in which the experiencer (Taijasa) creates and experiences subtle objects through the subtle body alone; fully real within its own domain (Prātibhāsika); not mere illusion, not the unconscious, not a lesser reality than waking.
* [Suṣupti](sushupti.md) - The deep dreamless sleep state — the third of the three states (Avasthātraya) — in which the experiencer (Prājña) rests in undifferentiated bliss without any object-awareness; not unconsciousness, not anesthesia, not a state of ignorance — the nearest natural approximation to non-dual experience available to the ordinary person.
* [Turīya](turiya.md) - The fourth — not a fourth experiential state alongside Jāgrat, Svapna, and Suṣupti, but the ever-present witnessing ground in which all three states appear and disappear; pure consciousness without modification; Advaita's most commonly misrendered concept after Mithyā.
* [Sākṣin](sakshin.md) - The witness consciousness — the self-luminous, uninvolved, permanent awareness in which all experience (thoughts, perceptions, states) appears as an object; the functional expression of Turīya in the presence of the three states; not the observer, not the ego watching itself, not a meditator, not introspection.

## Tier 4 — Nature of Brahman

* [Saguṇa Brahman](saguna-brahman.md) - Brahman as approached through qualities and attributes — the form of Brahman known as Īśvara (the Lord) — worshipped, invoked, and related to through devotion, prayer, and cosmological attribution; not a lower form of Brahman, not a lesser God, not in conflict with Nirguṇa Brahman (deferred from v0.4 bhakti-marga).
* [Nirguṇa Brahman](nirguna-brahman.md) - Brahman without attributes — Brahman as it is in itself, prior to all relational description, not characterized by any quality, not an object of experience, not the negation of Saguṇa Brahman — the Pāramārthika (ultimate) description of the one non-dual reality that the Mahāvākyas point to.
* [Antaryāmin](antaryamin.md) - The inner controller — Brahman as the immanent ruling principle within every being and object, dwelling inside yet unknown to what it dwells in, constituting its innermost Ātman — the primary Upaniṣadic formulation of Saguṇa Brahman's immanence; not the conscience, not the Holy Spirit, not a soul residing inside the body.
* [Sat-Cit-Ānanda](sat-chit-ananda.md) - Being-Consciousness-Bliss — the threefold Svarūpa Lakṣaṇa (own-nature indicator) of Brahman/Ātman; not three properties Brahman 'has' but what Brahman IS; not a compound noun for a state of experience; each term is non-different from Brahman and from each other.
* [Lakṣaṇa (Brahman)](laksana-brahman.md) - Indicator / pointer — the Vedāntic distinction between Svarūpa Lakṣaṇa (own-nature indicator, pointing to Brahman's essential nature) and Taṭastha Lakṣaṇa (accidental indicator, pointing to Brahman through a contingent relation like being the cause of the world); not Aristotelian essential vs. accidental properties; the distinction governs how the Mahāvākyas are to be interpreted.

## Tier 5 — Self, Sheath, Liberation

* [Upādhi](upadhi.md) - Limiting adjunct — a superimposed condition that makes the unlimited appear as though limited, without actually modifying what it limits; the technical term for the mechanism by which Brahman/Ātman appears to take on the characteristics of the body-mind complex without actually becoming limited; not illusion, not veil, not a layer covering the self.
* [Pañcakośa](pancha-kosha.md) - The five sheaths — the Taittirīya Upaniṣad's model of the apparent layers of the individual, from the physical body through food, breath, mind, intellect, and bliss — used in Vedāntic inquiry as a systematic framework for identifying what the Ātman is not; not a layered anatomy of the soul, not the Ātman in five modes, not a hierarchy of better-selves.
* [Ānandamaya Kośa](anandamaya-kosha.md) - The bliss sheath — the fifth and innermost of the Pañcakośa (five sheaths), corresponding to the causal body and the Suṣupti (deep sleep) state; the sheath nearest to the Ātman but still Anātman (not-self); not the Ātman itself, not the highest self, not the same as Brahman's Ānanda.
* [Jīvanmukti](jivanmukti.md) - Liberation while living — the Advaita Vedānta position that complete liberation from Avidyā (ignorance) and its results is possible while the body still functions; the Jīvanmukta has recognized the non-dual nature of the Ātman and continues to operate in the world without being bound by it; not enlightenment in the Buddhist sense, not a permanent altered state, not moral perfection.
* [Videhamukti](videhamukti.md) - Liberation upon the dissolution of the body — the state of the Jīvanmukta after Prārabdha Karma is exhausted and the physical body is relinquished; not death in the conventional sense, not heaven or an afterlife realm, not a superior liberation to Jīvanmukti — the same liberation minus the Prārabdha vehicle.
* [Brahmabhāvanā](brahmabhavana.md) - The active cultivation of Brahman-identity — the deliberate orienting of awareness toward 'I am Brahman' as the operative recognition during Nididhyāsana; the phase of practice that dissolves Viparīta-bhāvanā (contrary-to-knowledge habituation) by repeated, sustained Brahman-identification; not affirmation, not positive thinking, not autosuggestion, not self-hypnosis.
